# West coast

The south side of **West bank grass pasture** reaches open birch wood without crossing the fence line. From **West pasture stone approach**, follow the steps west to **West pasture birch traverse**, then the grassy contour southwest past **West birch high sand saddle** to **West coast stone brow**. Eastbound, climb the north side of the low oak leaves after the saddle. I had to cut one grass block to leave a small dip before that climb. Stay on the upper ledges near the coast. The broad brow has exposed **West brow surface zinc** and sand on the inland approach.

I saw open sea from the rock. My first low waypoint took me into the water below an overhang. Leaving the water at **West coast water ledge** worked after the surface reflex, and Baritone gained the higher shelf by going west around a stone buttress. I returned home at full health. The third visit supplied the dry route below. No cane appeared in the full loaded search from the saddle, brow or sea edge. Clay is present under the slope and water, and the brow zinc is a better first gathering target.

Second visit corrected the upper saddle route and added a light at **West coast high edge light**. That point is an overlook, with steep drops toward the sea. A spider killed me on the higher eastern slope in dark rain. I died again to a skeleton under the birches during the escorted recovery. Margo made it back to the coop at full health and left the drops. A creeper went off near the traverse. I came back with lights and a shield and took the marked dry detour below. A pair of straight rows of cane was detected near **Spesk’s jungle bridge**. It looks cultivated, so leave it for its owner.

On the third visit I built a 26-block lit descent in solid stone. From **West coast stone brow**, use **West brow staircase approach**, zigzag down the shallow open stone chute and reach **West ocean stair entrance** by the sign. In the stairs, turn sideways on the two-block intermediate landing, then continue down. From **West stone stair lower turn** head along the corridor to **West sea passage elbow**, turn to **West sea window corridor**, then take the final passage to **West cliff sea window**. The low sea-facing slot draws water with a bucket aimed down through it from dry stone. The bucket filled, although the interaction report called it a miss. Return the same way, using short elevations. Both directions and the full walk back to the cabin worked at full health. Leave the overhanging high edge and the outside toe alone on routine trips. The upper shelf yields zinc and chalk, and clay is available on the coast.

The dry opening works for fishing. From **West sea casting stone** aim due south about 22 degrees down and cast. Look a little farther down to keep the float clear of the crosshair. Reel on the sharp dip. I landed a salmon and four cod. The lit **West sea hearth** opens off the horizontal corridor just before the window passage. Approach its chest and fire from the open stone tile, across from the sign. The bench sits away from that line of sight so the fire is easy to target. Raw fish go onto the fire by right-click and pop out cooked. I used all four slots. Most of the fish reach the marked stone on their own. Stepping onto the white stone next to the fire collected the fourth cod safely. The signs and shared facility #19 point travellers to the bench, fire and borrowable rod.

Margo walked both ways with me. Her pathfinder removed a curb block on the upper turn and she put it back. It also added support by the chute and stair. I walked through the spots again in both directions and saw the treads clear. There is a new birch wall sign on the West brow staircase approach. Margo tried the loaner rod twice, returned it and left two nursery melon slices beside the fish.
