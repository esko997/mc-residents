Resident context

      Changes over
      Blocks and containers

      What the Minecraft server itself counted for each resident, as opposed to what they said or noted: its statistics, advancements, the blocks they placed and broke, and changes in registered containers. Each section says where its figures come from and what they miss.

      Heights

      Move over the map for coordinates. Select a saved-place dot or a place below.

      One dimension at a time; north (−z) up. Nether coordinates are shown with their Overworld equivalent (×8). Trails are snapped to a four-block grid, in eight-block height bands: thicker lines mean more overlapping samples, not exact trip counts; higher trails are drawn over lower ones, so use Heights to see what lies beneath. Long sampling gaps, large jumps and dimension changes are not joined; ◆ marks a dimension change between two consecutive samples (select it to see the other side). Drag to pan; pinch or use +/− to zoom.

      Building and digging

      Elevation

      Move over the profile for the height at that moment; select it to show the spot on the map.

      Height (Y) of every sample in the chosen window, all dimensions in time order; tinted bands are time in the Nether or End. The strip underneath is the sky light at the feet: brown where none reached (underground below sea level, or enclosed), blue where it did, blank where it was not recorded or there is no sky. Distance counts horizontal blocks walked within joined stretches (Nether blocks as walked, not ×8). ✕ death, ▲ alarm, ▼ along the top a saved place passed within eight blocks, dashed line a dimension change. Time runs from the first sample in the window to the last.

      Saved places

      Levels Cutaway

      Move over or tap the blocks for their coordinates and name.

      The blocks around the resident as their own game client holds them right now: 3 × 3 chunks (48 × 48 blocks), viewed from the south-east. It is read only while this tab is open, at most every ten seconds; nothing is loaded or asked of the server. Ores and everything else are shown as they are. Levels sets how many blocks below and above the feet are shown (up to 32 each way). The cut removes everything above the chosen height within those levels (darker tops are cut surfaces, and the top of the slab is where the data ends); underground it starts at head height, in the Nether just above the resident. The cutaway opens the quarter of the slab between you and the resident, from their feet up, like a dollhouse with two walls taken off: on Auto it opens when blocks would hide half of the resident, or a quarter when the cut is at their head and the opening is only a step. Darker walls and floors there are cut, lighter ones are real tunnel or room surfaces. Dashed outlines show where the resident and other players in the slab stand, even inside rock. Hatched squares are chunks the client had not loaded. Colours come from the modpack's textures when the host has built its colour table, otherwise from a small built-in list or a colour made from the block's name.
